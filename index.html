I am an M.S. candidate in the YU–KIST cooperative graduate program,
jointly advised by Dr. Heeseung Choi at the Visual Intelligence Group, KIST
and Prof. Bumsub Ham at the Computer Vision Lab, Yonsei University.
My research is conducted at KIST. I am applying to Ph.D. programs for Fall 2027.

At KIST, I have worked on federated domain generalization and text-to-image retrieval for person re-identification, and on SkillLens, a multimodal proficiency-estimation framework accepted as an oral at the CVPR 2026 Workshop on MOMA. I hold a B.S. in Electrical and Electronic Engineering from Yonsei University, where my capstone research on open-vocabulary object detection with Junghyun Park received the Most Popular Award (3rd place) at the 2024 EE-Festival.

Research interests

My research aims to develop multimodal models that understand how agents interact with objects and predict the physical consequences of their actions.

Two questions guide this direction.

  1. 1

    How can multimodal models learn representations of physical scenes and human actions that generalize across environments?

  2. 2

    How can models use these representations to reason about the physical consequences of actions?

Research experience

  1. Research
    Robust Text-to-Image Person Re-Identification for Real-World Search
    Hyukjin Kim
    2026
    Ongoing (Aug. 2026 – present)
    Abs

    • Text-based person retrieval for missing-person and suspect searches where reference images are unavailable and only natural-language descriptions may be provided
    • Adding image-grounded attributes generated by a vision-language model produced only modest gains, which led to asking which textual details the model actually uses
    • Controlled attribute-ablation experiments on IRRA across CUHK-PEDES, RSTPReID, and ICFG-PEDES: removing upper-body clothing descriptions reduced Rank-1 by 31.6, 25.6, and 43.3 percentage points; the relative importance of garment color and type differed across datasets

  2. Research
    Multimodal Human Proficiency Estimation (SkillLens)
    Hyukjin Kim
    2026
    Apr. 2026 – Jun. 2026 · CVPR 2026 Workshop on MOMA (Oral)
    Abs Website

    • Studied how an action is performed, not just which action: whether local motion patterns capture proficiency differences that global video representations miss
    • Contributed core ideas and experimental design for action-guided Prototype Dynamics Margin (PDM) and CORAL-based ordinal calibration
    • Combined egocentric and exocentric video with gaze, using atomic-action descriptions as temporal guidance; 54.2% accuracy on Ego-Exo4D, outperforming the strongest evaluated prior baseline (50.1%) by 4.1 percentage points

Capstone

  1. Capstone
    Improving Open-Vocabulary Object Detection via Knowledge Distillation
    Hyukjin Kim and Junghyun Park
    2024
    3rd place, Most Popular Award @ EE-Festival
    Abs PDF

    • Baseline: BARON (Aligning Bag of Regions for open-vocabulary object detection)
    • Identified a mismatch between teacher and student similarity structures for bags of regions sampled around the same region proposal
    • Applied similarity-preserving knowledge distillation to align teacher–student relational representations
    • Improved novel-class box AP50 on COCO by +1.2 points (23.1 → 24.3)
